# RIFF.md — the commentary, and the evidence under each line

Riff is commentary led by evidence, not opinion laid over pictures. Each line
below is something the narration says *about* what is on screen rather than
describing it, with the thing that entitles it to be said.

---

## The through-line

**A pitch is a set of claims about a thing that does not exist yet, made by
someone whose only evidence is a thing that does.** So the whole film is
organised around one question a reviewer should be asking the entire time:
*which of these sentences could this person actually be held to?*

That is why every card carries a status band, why two beats are real played
capture rather than a mock-up, and why the film spends twenty-two seconds —
nearly a tenth of its length — on a list of things that **have not been
started**.

## The riffs, in order

**B01 — "a pitch that fabricates progress has already failed."**
This is the assignment's own rule said out loud in the first fifteen seconds,
and it is said *before* any claim is made, so the rest of the film is
committed to it. The hesitant writer types *finished* and corrects it to
*proposed* while the sentence is still on screen, which is the shape of the
whole thing in one gesture.

**B03 — "two systems that never touch."**
The temptation in a pitch is to say the comparable is worse. This does not.
Hollow Knight does both halves well and the film says so; the claim is only
that it keeps them apart. **A comparison that insists the other game is bad
is a market claim with no evidence**, and the assignment forbids those by
name. A comparison about structure can be argued with, which is what makes it
worth making.

**B04 — "nothing staged."**
Said over footage whose every frame came through the real input map, with the
log on the card. The riff is not *look how it moves*; it is *you are allowed
to check this*, and the next line gives you the revision to check it at.

**B06 — "watch what it hits."**
The one deliberately theatrical beat, and it earns it because the answer is
**nothing**. A hitbox that has been built, measured, pinned at three frame
rates and filmed, and has never touched anything, is the most honest thing
this project owns. It is also exactly why §5's first piece is an enemy: the
film makes the argument by showing the hole rather than by describing it.

**B06 → B07 — the cut.**
The narration runs straight from *watch what it hits* into *nothing, because
there is no enemy and there never has been*, and lands in the two lists. That
is the film's hinge: the last thing you see working is the thing that proves
the rest is missing.

**B07 — "ten things can be cloned at a tag, and the eleventh is in no
repository at all."**
The eleventh is the design document. An earlier cut said *eleven things can be
cloned at a tag* while the table beside it marked that row as a gap.
`FACTCHECK.md` records the correction, because a film about honest accounting
getting its own count wrong is the exact failure it is warning about.

**B08 — "the same number, out of the same file."**
The riff is that the first test of unbuilt work is **already written**, in the
sense that the number it will assert is already measured and committed. Most
plans say *and then I will test it*. This one names the assertion and the file
it comes out of, and a reviewer can read that file today.

**B09 — "a room with nothing in it."**
The strongest line in the film is about the weakness of its own evidence. Two
hundred and eighty checks, and the most-asserted rule in the project has never
met an opponent. **The riff is not that the rule is wrong — it is that nobody
can know.** The card's status band says `PENDING` rather than `proposed`,
because a proposal is a decision and this is a question.

**B10 — "I have never made it meet anything."**
Placed at the end of the learning list so it reads as a summary rather than as
self-deprecation. It is also literally true and countable: zero enemy scenes,
zero boss scenes, no export.

**B11 — what stayed the author's.**
The verdict names the split instead of implying it. AI organised the sections,
hashed the evidence and ran the checks. Scope, the comparable, the role and
whether any of this is worth playing were decided in one line by a person, and
`PROMPTS.md` quotes that line.

## What the film deliberately does NOT riff on

- **How the game looks.** Two captures, no adjectives. The art is from a
  previous assignment and this film is not arguing about it.
- **The shaders and particles**, beyond naming their tags. They are evidence
  of skill here, not subject matter; A5 and A6 are their films.
- **Anything a viewer cannot check.** No invented player, no playtest, no
  metric, no wishlist, no studio. There is one frame-rate-shaped number in the
  whole film — 36 physics frames — and it is arithmetic on a clip length, not
  a performance measurement.

## The one thing the riff cannot do

It cannot tell you whether the purification beat lands. The film says so, in
B10, and leaves it as the open row rather than closing it with a sentence
nobody measured.
